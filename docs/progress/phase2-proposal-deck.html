02 · Why nowRamachandra Ortho Care · Phase II
Design principles

Why this is the right next step, and how it's being built

Phase II isn't a rebuild. It's the same system, taught to remember. Every screen below follows the same three rules Phase I was built on.

02
1

Doctor time is the scarce resource

Every screen is built to be filled in seconds, not minutes. Templates, favourites and autosave do the typing. If it slows the consultation down, it doesn't ship.

2

Reception stays in control of the room

Front desk keeps full access to booking, billing and the queue. Only the clinical write actions, diagnosis and prescriptions, are gated to the doctor.

3

Nothing leaves the clinic without asking

Patient data stays behind a login. Reports and prescriptions reach the patient only through a signed link or the patient's own portal, never a public URL.

What "connected" actually means here

Today, a returning patient's fracture follow-up starts from zero. Dr. Ramachandra re-asks what was already found last visit. With Phase II, opening that patient's file surfaces the last consultation, the X-ray that was uploaded, and the medicines already tried, before he says a word.

The same record that the doctor writes becomes the prescription that prints, the invoice that reception bills, and the update the patient receives on WhatsApp, written once and used four times instead of retyped at every desk.

12 · Looking aheadRamachandra Ortho Care · Phase II
Not in this phase: the honest version

Where AI could help later, and where it never should

12
Doctor-led
AI support

What AI can eventually do

Draft a consultation summary from the doctor's shorthand for him to review and edit. Flag a patient overdue for a follow-up. Summarise a patient's history into one paragraph before a busy day starts.

What AI should never do

Suggest a diagnosis. Choose a medicine or dosage. Contact a patient with anything that reads as medical advice. Every clinical decision stays with Dr. Ramachandra, full stop. This is a boundary, not a limitation to work around later.

15 · Technical & trust appendixRamachandra Ortho Care · Phase II
New: appendix · 1 of 2

How patient data is actually protected

Written for the clinic, not for engineers: what's true today, and what Phase II adds.

15
🔐

Nothing is public by default

Patient records, prescriptions and reports sit behind row-level security in the database itself. Even if someone had the technical know-how to query it directly, they'd still need to be a recognised staff login to see a single record.

✅

Staff, not just "logged in"

A login alone proves nothing. Every check also confirms the email is on the clinic's own staff list, so a stranger who signs up on their own can never see patient data, only a recognised member of the team can.

💾

Backed up automatically

The database backs itself up daily without anyone needing to remember to do it. If a device is ever lost, the clinic's records are not.

🧾

Reports, never a bare link

X-rays and MRIs live in private storage. Every time one is opened, by staff or by a patient, a fresh, time-limited link is generated for that one view, then it expires.

15 · Technical & trust appendixRamachandra Ortho Care · Phase II
New: appendix · 2 of 2

Who owns the data, and what happens if something goes wrong

15
🏥

The clinic owns everything

Every patient record, prescription and report belongs to Ramachandra Ortho Care, not to made., and not to any third party. If the clinic ever wanted to move to different software, the data is exportable, not locked in.

🛠

If something breaks

The same reliability watchdog pattern already running for Phase I extends to the new record-keeping. A fault is caught and flagged, not discovered days later when someone goes looking for a missing file.

📜

An audit trail that can't be edited

The activity log on page 17 is append-only by design: entries can be added, never rewritten or deleted, so it stands up as a real record of who did what and when.

🇮🇳

Built for how Indian clinics actually run

WhatsApp for reminders because that's where patients already are. Phone-number login because that's the credential nobody forgets. No assumption that every patient has an email or a smartphone app.
